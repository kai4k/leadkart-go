# ADR 0073 — Inactive, never deleted: products, roles and drug schedule classes

**Status:** Accepted (owner decision, 2026-11-24).
**Date:** 2026-11-24
**Extends:** [ADR 0071](0071-data-is-retained-erasure-is-deferred.md) (people and companies were made active or inactive; this does the same for the business records that still had a delete route). **Amends:** [ADR 0061](0061-inventory-module-aggregates-and-events.md) (the product soft delete and its `product_has_live_stock` guard), [ADR 0069](0069-rank-hierarchy-and-grant-authority.md) (the role delete and its permission `identity.roles.delete`), [ADR 0070](0070-company-commerce-settings-module.md) (removing a drug schedule class). Those ADRs stay as written.

## Context

The owner's words: *"All become Inactive."* LeadKart never deletes a business record. A soft delete that is only labelled "delete" does not count: the row stays but the record disappears from every read, its name is freed, and nothing can bring it back. Three resources still had a delete route:

* **Products:** `DELETE /inventory/products/{id}` hid the product everywhere (`is_deleted`) once no live batch held stock.
* **Roles:** `DELETE /roles/{id}` hid the role (`is_deleted`) once no ACTIVE member held it, and revoked its assignments from the INACTIVE holders.
* **Drug schedule classes:** `DELETE /company/drug-schedule-classes/{id}` removed the class from the company's list once no product carried it.

Nothing is live, so there is no backfill, no compatibility shim and no legacy tolerance; git keeps the old code.

## Decision

1. **One lifecycle for all three: active or inactive, both directions.** Each gets `POST .../deactivate` and `POST .../reactivate`, both answering `200` with the updated record (ADR 0038), both idempotent (repeating one is a no-op: no event, and for a class no new policy version). The transition is a method of the aggregate (`Product.Deactivate` / `Activate`, `Role.Deactivate` / `Activate`, `Settings.DeactivateScheduleClass` / `ReactivateScheduleClass`), each emitting its own domain event into the outbox (`ProductDeactivatedV1` / `ProductReactivatedV1`, `RoleDeactivatedV1` / `RoleReactivatedV1`, and a class change is a new company-policy version with its reason in the history). The delete routes, the `SoftDeleted` / `Deleted` events and `ProductSoftDeletedV1` / `RoleDeletedV1` are removed.
2. **Lists take `?status=active|inactive|all` (default `active`)** and return `status_counts {active, inactive}` (whatever the filter is; on the first page only for the paginated product list), on the one vocabulary `internal/common/lifecycle` that the members list already used. Anything else is 400 `invalid_status_filter`.
3. **The soft-delete columns go.** `inventory.products` and `identity.roles` lose `is_deleted`, `deleted_at`, `deleted_by`; roles gain `is_active` (products already had it). The partial indexes that carried `WHERE NOT is_deleted` are rebuilt. A record deleted before the migration becomes INACTIVE, never active again. A SKU is unique over every product of the company, inactive ones included. (A role name was first ruled the same; the 2026-11-25 addendum below frees role and drug schedule class names.)
4. **PATCH no longer toggles `is_active`** on a product (one way to change status, with its own event).

## What "inactive" means, per resource

| | Inactive means | Unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | no new batch (`409 product_inactive`); the reorder alert skips it | readable, editable, its batches and stock, every existing order and invoice line, the expiry alerts (stock still expires) |
| **Role** | confers nothing (no permission, no rank); no new holder (`409 role_inactive` on assign); not editable; not a parent; hidden from the default list | readable (`GET`), its assignments, its history |
| **Drug schedule class** | cannot be newly set on a product (`422 errors.drug_schedule: [inactive]`) | a product that already carries it keeps it, and its label still resolves; renaming still works |

**An order or quotation line for an inactive product is not blocked here.** Orders and quotations carry a `product_id` string and nothing in Orders (or anywhere) validates it against the catalogue today (lines arrive with round G5). The rule "an inactive product cannot be added to a new order or quotation" has nothing to attach to yet; it belongs to G5 and needs a catalogue port into Orders. Reported, not built.

## Roles: what delete did, and what deactivate does (security)

What delete did to the members who held the role (read from `DeleteRoleHandler`, `MayDeleteRole` and the authority-facts loaders, not guessed):

* the caller needed authority over the role AND over every holder, and the ceiling over every permission the role carries (`MayDeleteRole`, answered before the in-use rule);
* a role with an ACTIVE holder was refused, `409 role_in_use`, nothing changed; so a delete never removed permissions from an active member;
* with only INACTIVE holders left, the role was marked deleted and their assignments were revoked in the same transaction;
* a deleted role conferred nothing: every authority read skips it (`RankOf`, the permission resolver `GetByIDs`, `ListSuperAdminMembershipsInTenant`, the permission-holder query, `MayAssignRole`, `parentEligible`).

Deactivate keeps every one of those rules and loosens none:

* **Same decision, renamed:** `Actor.MayDeactivateRole` is `MayDeleteRole` unchanged, and the permission is renamed, not weakened: `identity.roles.delete` became `identity.roles.manage` with exactly the same holders (the `tenant.admin` implication set and the owner-only ceiling). The web's catalogue changes by that one name.
* **Same in-use rule:** an active holder is `409 role_in_use`.
* **An inactive role confers nothing**, through the same reads a deleted role was skipped by (`IsActive` replaces `IsDeleted` in each). SQL that decided who holds a permission or a super-admin role filters `is_active`.
* **One difference, tighter on data and equal on authority:** the assignments of the INACTIVE holders are kept (nothing is deleted). Consequence: such a member may be reactivated while the role is inactive; they receive nothing from it (proved end to end). When the role is reactivated they get it back, which is why reactivating asks `MayReactivateRole`: the deactivate authority (over the role, the ceiling over its permissions, every holder active or not) PLUS what every conferral asks, the role-tier rule and a durable hold of each of the role's permissions (a time-bound holder cannot make a permission permanent for others this way), and it rotates every active holder's security stamp so their tokens carry the restored permissions at once.
* A race between deactivating and assigning is serialised by the tenant authority lock: whichever runs second sees the other's result (the assign is refused `role_inactive`, or the deactivate `role_in_use`), so that race never leaves an active member holding an inactive role (asserted in the race e2e). An active member CAN hold an inactive role by another path: a holder who was inactive when the role was deactivated is later reactivated. That member receives nothing from the role.

## Consequences

* The web: remove the three delete actions; add Deactivate / Reactivate; add the status chips and `status=` filter to the three lists; rename `identity.roles.delete` to `identity.roles.manage` in its permission catalogue; `RoleDto.is_active`, `ScheduleClassDto.active`; `PATCH /inventory/products` no longer takes `is_active`. The route table is in `docs/BFF_API_CHANGES.md` section 4e.
* Quotations already follow this: a draft is cancelled with `POST /orders/quotations/{id}/reject`, which keeps the row (state `rejected`); nothing to build.
* Batches are never deleted either (controller ruling on the owner's never-delete rule): `Batch.SoftDelete` (called only by domain tests; no handler, route or job ever used it), `ErrDeleted` and the `inventory.batches` columns `is_deleted`, `deleted_at`, `deleted_by` are removed (migration 20261125000007; a batch deleted earlier is visible again, a duplicate batch number takes its id). Stock is corrected by movements: write-off, quarantine, adjustment. The arch gate covers the batch table model and domain type.
* A stored company policy written before this change has no `active` on its drug schedule classes and would read every class as inactive; there is no live data, so no shim was written.

## Fitness function

`TestArch_InactiveNotDeleted_NoDeleteRouteOnTheThreeResources` (in `internal/architecture/`) fails when a module registers a `DELETE` route for a product, a role or a drug schedule class, or when the product or role table models carry a soft-delete column again. `TestArch_RouteHasSpecOperation` keeps `api/openapi.yaml` and the routes in step, and the per-resource end-to-end tests in `cmd/api/inactive_not_deleted_e2e_test.go` (deactivate, hidden from the default list, shown by `status=inactive`, the effect enforced, reactivate) plus the role security proofs in `role_authority_e2e_test.go` carry the behaviour.

## Addendum 2026-11-25: names free up on delete; SKUs never do

Owner rulings, on top of "inactive, never deleted":

1. **The web shows the action as Delete.** Behind the scenes it stays a state change (inactive). A deleted record is gone from the screen and the web offers no restore. The reactivate routes of the Go API stay for support and operations tooling.
2. **Names free up on delete.** A role name and a drug schedule class label are unique among ACTIVE rows only, so a new "Area Manager" can be created after the old one was deleted. Migration `20261125000008` makes `identity.uq_roles_tenant_name` partial (`WHERE is_active`). The drug schedule class list lives in the company policy document, not a table, so its label rule is in the domain (`commercesettings`, `commercepolicy.Validate`), over active classes only. Reactivating a role or class whose name an active one now holds is refused: `409 role_name_taken` / `409 schedule_class_name_taken`, nothing changes (the repository, backed by the partial unique index, decides, so a concurrent create cannot slip through).
3. **SKUs never free up.** A product SKU stays unique over every product of the company (old invoices print it). When a create collides with an INACTIVE product's SKU the answer is `409 product_sku_taken_by_inactive`, so the web can say "A deleted product used this SKU. Pick another."; an active holder keeps `409 already_exists`. A product SKU cannot be edited, so only create can collide.
4. **A role is deleted only when nobody holds it.** The in-use rule counted ACTIVE holders only; it now counts every holder, active or inactive. `409 role_in_use` carries `holders`, `holder_names` (up to five display names) and `errors.active_holders`. The earlier note that an inactive holder keeps an assignment to an inactive role no longer arises: the assignment has to be revoked before the role can go inactive.
5. **Roles are written by LeadKart only.** `identity.roles.create`, `identity.roles.update` and `identity.roles.manage` leave the ADR 0068 `tenant.admin` mapping in effect: no tenant-plane grant satisfies them (explicit, implied by `tenant.admin`, or carried by a super-admin role minted outside the platform tenant), whoever in the company holds it. They are satisfied only by LeadKart platform authority: the `platform.roles.manage` equivalent for a platform operator acting on a company (`X-Tenant-Id`), an impersonation session (a platform operator acting inside the company), and a platform super user. The platform tiers that hold role management are `platform.roles.manage`, the super user of the platform tenant, impersonation, and LeadKart's own platform-tenant staff acting inside the platform tenant (a slug-anchored platform operator whose own tenant grant, explicit or implied by `tenant.admin`, counts, as it did before the ruling: `tenantPlaneMayHold` returns true for an impersonation session or a platform operator). None was added; the platform-tenant tier was briefly lost in the first cut of this rule and restored (G2l). `identity.roles.assign`, `revoke` and `view` are unchanged, and every other authority rule (rank, `grant_exceeds_authority`, the role-edit ceilings) is untouched: they now bind the platform actor, and a company administrator still meets them when assigning. The decision lives in one place, `authn.satisfiesIn` (`permission.IsPlatformManagedRole`), so the route gate and `effective_permissions` agree. This is an authority rule, so it is recorded here beside the other 2026-11-25 rulings rather than in its own ADR. 

   The rule is also carried in the domain, not only at the route gate. The authority `Actor` has a `MayManageRoles` fact (`authn.AuthorityFrom`: the caller satisfies a role-management name through platform authority), and `MayCreateRole` and every role-mutating decision (edit, replace, grant, revoke, re-parent, deactivate, reactivate) refuse without it (`no authority`), so a future route or job behind a different gate cannot let a company write a role. Without the fact the three names are neither listed nor implied by `tenant.admin` in the actor's effective set, `mayConferManaged` and `mayChangePermissions` refuse to confer them (a role, an override, an approval, an assign of a role that carries them: `grant_exceeds_authority`), and they are no longer requestable (`permission.IsRequestable`: 422 `permission_not_requestable`, absent from the approver queue). The JWT `permission` claim minted at login and refresh drops them for a member of any tenant other than the platform tenant, so `/auth/me/capabilities` `permissions` (the raw claim) agrees with `effective_permissions`. They stay in `ImpliedByTenantAdmin`, because an impersonation session reaches them through it.
5a. **The three names exist only in the platform tenant (G2m).** They mean nothing outside it (the claim drops them, the gate ignores them), so no actor, LeadKart included (operator, impersonation session, super user), may confer them on a COMPANY role or a company member's override (`POST/PUT` role permissions, role create, role reactivation, role assign, `PATCH /users/{id}/permission-overrides`): `403 grant_exceeds_authority` (`authority.mayConferManaged`). Inside the platform tenant they stay conferrable by an actor holding the role-write fact from there. They are also not requestable inside the platform tenant any more (`permission.IsRequestable` is global; a platform-tenant member could request them before G2k): they are conferred by role or override by LeadKart only. REMOVING them from a COMPANY is never refused by the grant ceiling: revoking a role that carries one, removing such an override, replacing a role's permissions without it, deactivating such a role, taking one off a member's revoked list. A company owner can therefore always clear an inert name an earlier build left behind. G2n: putting a managed name ON a company member's revoked list counts as conferring (refused like a grant; nothing managed lands on a company override, granted or revoked), and lifting a stale one counts as a removal. Inside the platform tenant the names are real authority, so there removal (and lifting a revoke) is NOT exempt and still needs the ceiling. G2o: "the platform tenant" is a fact about the TARGET tenant, never about who the actor is (`inPlatformTenant`: the target equals the platform tenant id the actor was built with, `ActorInput.PlatformTenantID`). G2p: `authn.AuthorityFrom` sets that id for EVERY caller, whichever tenant its token names. It is the id of the tenant with slug `platform`, read from the database by a resolver the composition root installs (`authn.PlatformTenant`, `ports.NewPlatformTenantResolver`, cached once found), never from the token or the request; only if the lookup fails does a platform-slug token fall back to its own tenant. A session or a company super user acting in the platform tenant therefore gets the platform rule, so a session cannot lift a staff member's managed revoke (the G2n review B1). The `NewActor` default (operator with no id is in its own tenant) remains for tests only. On top of that, opening an impersonation session whose target is the platform tenant is refused (`422 impersonation_invalid`; `CreateImpersonationSession`): LeadKart staff act in their own tenant directly, and a session there would hold `tenant.admin` over it with no platform permission. Where the id is unknown (the platform tenant lookup failed and the token names a company), the actor is treated as acting in a company; only a platform operator can scope into another tenant, so a company super user acting in the platform tenant is unreachable by any route today, and now classified correctly anyway. "Never refused" for company removals means never refused by the grant ceiling: rank, `MayActOn` and the Admin tier for lifting a revoke still apply. Reactivating a role runs the full ceiling over the names it confers (`mayChange`); after the platform-tenant narrowing this is the same decision `MayDeactivateRole` already makes, kept explicit so the conferral path cannot lose its `holds` check. The role-write fact itself is decided per TARGET tenant in the domain (`mayManageRolesIn`): an operator holding only `platform.users.manage` is exempt in a company for member repair but may not write its roles, matching the route gate, which needs `platform.roles.manage` to reach another tenant.
6. **Known and accepted (info).** Role-write authority is reachable by any operator holding `platform.impersonation.manage` (an impersonation session is Admin-equivalent and carries `tenant.admin`), not only by `platform.roles.manage`. Unchanged from before the ruling.
